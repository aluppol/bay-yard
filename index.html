Questions

The ones people ask before they call.

How much does a service visit cost?

Service visits start at $100. That covers coming out, inspecting the system and telling you what is wrong. Larger installations and new systems are quoted with a free estimate before any work begins.

Do you charge for an estimate?

Not for larger installations and new irrigation systems — those get a free estimate first. A service visit to diagnose a fault on an existing system is the $100 visit, because the diagnosis is the work.

My sprinklers will not turn on. What is usually wrong?

Usually one of four things: the controller has lost its program or its power, a valve solenoid has failed, the wiring between the controller and the valve has corroded or broken, or the line itself is damaged. They look identical from the driveway, which is why the system gets inspected before anything is replaced.

My system is old. Do I need to replace all of it?

Often not. Bay Yard works on both older irrigation systems and modern smart controllers, and can improve an existing system instead of replacing everything when replacement is not necessary.

Can I get a rebate for this work?

Often, yes. Santa Clara Valley Water runs rebates for water-efficient irrigation — converting spray to in-line drip, upgrading to a weather-based controller, high-efficiency nozzles and rain sensors — plus $2 per square foot for replacing lawn. Single-family households can claim up to $3,000.

Apply and wait for their written Notice to Proceed before any work starts or any materials are bought, because the programme does not pay retroactively. Current funding runs to 30 June 2027. Check what your address qualifies for at valleywater.org.

Do you install smart controllers?

Yes. Weather-based controllers adjust watering to the weather instead of running a fixed schedule, and they are one of the items Valley Water rebates. An existing system usually does not need replacing to take one.
